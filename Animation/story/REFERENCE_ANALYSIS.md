## Narration and explanatory structure

The rough full-track ASR and visual sequence agree on this **paraphrased** arc: two companies race to finish a deep-canyon railway; a young engineer leaves gaps; the boss objects; the rival joins rails tightly and initially offers a smoother ride; reputation suffers on the noisy line; summer heat closes the engineer's gaps while continuous rival rails push against one another and buckle. Do not treat rough recognition as evidence for exact names, measurements or quoted dialogue.

The core structure is **apparent flaw → pressure to remove it → rival's apparent success → environmental change → hidden physical reason → reversal/payoff**. Narration carries connective information while props/actions demonstrate it. Short captions emphasize current phrases rather than explaining an unrelated graphic.

**Physics caveat:** this is a dramatized explanation of expansion allowances, not a universal claim that modern continuous welded rail cannot work. Real railways use stress management, fastening/ballast systems, temperature procedures and other engineering provisions. Future factual scripts must be researched separately; do not copy a spectacular crash as a complete engineering lesson.

## Art direction: characters AND world

### Characters

- White/pale round faces; confident dark outlines; compact simple bodies and thin articulated limbs.
- Hat, hair, coat/vest color and silhouette distinguish roles without photorealistic anatomy.
- Readable eyebrows, eyes and mouths; face-color anger and steam exaggerate emotion.
- Work, pointing, walking, arguing, reacting and falling are motivated by specific objects and events.
- Multiple story participants and background people—not a fixed presenter/audience pair.

### Environments and objects

- Layered warm ochre rock, cool canyon depth and sky; strong silhouette separation.
- Ballast, sleepers, rail metal, timber beams, cranes, station glass/roofing, curtains and lamps create a material-rich world.
- Bridge supports and repeated track elements establish depth and continuity.
- Ground contact and shadows prevent characters/props from floating.
- Rail gaps, coins, calendar, thermometer and newspaper are **story mechanisms**, not decorative clip art.
- The same world has several locations and states: construction, station, carriage, seasonal heat, bridge climax. The new request therefore cannot remain classroom-only.

### Camera, motion and captions

- Establish → medium interaction → reaction → insert/extreme close-up → return to wide.
- Follow the character, tool, rail join or train. Camera changes explain where to look and why.
- Layer parallax, depth, scale changes and traversing motion sell 3D space while faces remain illustrative.
- Motion is continuous inside a shot: work actions, locomotion, train movement, rail deformation and expressive reactions. Replacing the picture every few seconds is not an equivalent animation system.
- Bold white/cream phrase captions with dark outlines stay low-center. Protect faces, rail-gap demonstrations and platform UI areas; test actual text at delivery resolution.

## What this implementation transfers—and what it does not

| Reference principle | Working source in this extension | Honest limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Story-specific location | Six world foundations; free-positioned dimensional primitives and custom colors | Not an automatic environment generator or the reference's detailed set dressing |
| Story cast | Up to 30 cast entries, costumes/colors/hats, per-scene placement and acting | Shared base cutout rig; new silhouettes/creatures need authored rig code |
| Physical props and cause/effect | Tracks/gaps/heat/buckling, train/wheels/steam, bridge/crane, garden growth, generic geometry | Stylized deterministic effects, not structural engineering or rigid-body simulation |
| Motivated camera | Start/end target and zoom; continuous ease, hard cut between scenes | Fixed oblique projection; no orbit/lens/DOF system in this baseline |
| English narration | Complete local TTS + per-scene imported voice option | Baseline voice is synthetic; not reference-quality voice acting |
| Captions and assembly | Measured scene-audio timing, phrase chunks, burned captions, SRT, H264/AAC | Phrase timing inside each clip is estimated, not phoneme/word forced alignment |

The reference is a directing target, **not a claim that the compact procedural demos match its polish, camera freedom or asset complexity**.
