# Historical accuracy and editorial ledger

Prepared 2026-11-20. Sources below were retrieved during this production. Their modern retrospective accounts can disagree; this film does not pretend otherwise. No archival pictures or audio from these pages are used in the animation.

## Sources consulted

**S1 — City of Boston Archives, “100 years ago today: Molasses crashes through Boston's North End.”**
https://www.boston.gov/news/100-years-ago-today-molasses-crashes-through-bostons-north-end

Retrieved text: tank 50 ft tall; 2.3 million gallons; built 1915; children collected leaks; brown paint to conceal; thin/unsuitable steel; police, naval cadets and Red Cross rescue; 21 killed / 150 injured; saltwater cleanup with fireboat; 1925 court-appointed auditor held owners' negligence responsible. Its account says an elevated train was knocked off the tracks and places the event at about 1 p.m. Those details differ from S2.

**S2 — Edwards Park, Smithsonian, “Without Warning, Molasses Surged Over Boston 100 Years Ago,” originally November 1983, republished January 14, 2019.**
https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/without-warning-molasses-january-surged-over-boston-180971251/

Retrieved all three chunks. Chunk 1 describes approximately 12:30, 50 ft tall / 90 ft diameter, 2.3 million gallons, a 15 ft wave and estimated 35 mph. Chunk 2 explicitly describes saltwater hosing, sand on the streets and the inner harbor turning brown. A tank fragment knocked out an El upright; an **approaching train stopped as the track sagged**. It describes 21 deaths, 150 injuries, difficult rescue, a six-year legal process and inadequate safety factor. This is the specific railway sequence followed visually and in the edited narration.

**S3 — Julia Press, NPR, “A Deadly Tsunami Of Molasses In Boston's North End,” January 15, 2019.**
https://www.npr.org/2019/01/15/685154620/a-deadly-tsunami-of-molasses-in-bostons-north-end

Retrieved main article. Interviews historian Stephen Puleo, engineer Ronald Mayville and science educator Nicole Sharp. Supports 50 ft, 2.3 million gallons, 21/150, leaky under-designed tank, brown paint, cooling/thickening molasses, and expert testimony/oversight implications. Uses **30 ft** for the wave, illustrating uncertainty versus S2's 15 ft. Mayville says there is **no surefire single reason** for the exact moment of failure, while under-design/thin steel are clear. Industrial alcohol/rum and wartime munitions context appear in the article.

## Exact spoken train edits

`script-supplied.txt` retains the pre-edit narrative working text. `script.txt`, `audio/takes.json`, captions and voice takes use:

1. **Before:** “It smashed buildings, swept away horses, crushed people, and even knocked a train off its tracks.”
   **After:** “It smashed buildings, swept away horses, crushed people, and even damaged an elevated railway.”
2. **Before:** “Even a passing elevated train was knocked off its tracks.”
   **After:** “The elevated railway was damaged, and an approaching train stopped short of the sagging tracks.”

No other body-text wording is intentionally changed by the editorial step. Whitespace, paragraph boundaries and the title are not spoken. See the project tests for a mechanical comparison of these two working text files. This comparison checks the saved production inputs, not a separately archived upload of the original chat message.

## Reconstruction boundaries

- Tank plate separation, shell compression, flood-front motion and floating boards are explanatory keyframed geometry, **not simulated failure/flow predictions**.
- Tank and neighborhood sit in a coherent depth scene, but exact street/building locations and dimensions are not surveyed.
- The commemorative slab/candles are our original design, not the wording or appearance of the actual modern Boston marker.
- People are generic; no named survivor actions, invented quotes, facial likenesses or imagined last moments are attributed to real victims.
- No footage from the Dario reference, historical websites or prior ship project is composited into the film. The Dario clip governs style and camera treatment only.
